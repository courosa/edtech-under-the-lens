ABOUT THE PROJECT

What is this?

A set of playful writing and discussion activities for thinking critically about educational technology.

Write. Question. Share.

Work on your own or with a small group to write a fictional corporate apology, pitch a familiar classroom tool, or imagine an exhibit in a future museum. Brainstorm, put your ideas into words, and share what you notice.

The aim is to examine the promises tools make, the assumptions behind them, and their effects on learners and teachers. Drawing parallels between familiar classroom technologies and newer tools helps us ask what has changed and which questions keep returning.

The humour opens the conversation. The thinking comes from considering different experiences, questioning easy claims, and explaining what meaningful learning might require.

Your ideas do the work

These are writing and brainstorming exercises, followed by sharing and conversation. The site does not use AI to generate, evaluate, or score your responses. It simply arranges the words you write into a piece you can present or download.

You do not need an account or any AI tools. Drafts stay in your browser; they are not submitted to an instructor or shared automatically. You can edit the briefs, return to your answers, or clear your saved work.

Make it work for your group

Use the activities as they are, adapt a brief to your context, or choose the prompts and discussion questions that best suit your group. They can support a class, a professional learning session, or a conversation among colleagues.

Where it began

Originally created by Dr. Alec Couros for EC&I 833: Foundations of Educational Technology, a graduate course at the University of Regina. The activities were designed to introduce students to critical analysis of educational technology through writing, brainstorming, and sharing.
